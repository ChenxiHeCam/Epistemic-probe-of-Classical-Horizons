# EPOCH: Epistemic Probe of Classical Horizons

This repository accompanies **"Understanding where physical theories fail without knowing what replaces them"** (Chenxi He and Jingqiu Chen).
EPOCH tests whether a body of physical knowledge bounded by a date still
accounts for a set of measurements, where that compatibility ends, and whether
a prediction of the theory that historically succeeded it is compatible with the same data.

Terms used throughout this repository:

- The **knowledge horizon** is a calendar date, here 1899. A relation is
  admissible if its physical content follows from laws and principles
  documented up to that date; rewriting a law in dimensionless or
  one-variable form adds no physical content and is allowed.
- A **point cloud** is the set of `(x, y)` pairs of one relation, normalized to
  remove scale and stripped of formula, units, labels and ordering.
- A **law family** is the set of point clouds derived from one physical law
  traced to one dated source; a **mechanism group** is the set of measured
  series produced by one physical mechanism. AUROCs are computed, and bootstrap
  intervals resampled, over families or groups.

EPOCH separates generic screens from tests of named predictions:

1. the **representation screen**, a neural encoder trained only on point
   clouds from pre-horizon relations, which scores a new point cloud by its
   distance from them (the learned score);
2. the **extrapolation screen**, which fits the best of power, exponential,
   linear and constant forms over a fitted regime and scores how far the rest
   of the data departs from the extrapolation; and
3. the **named-prediction test**, which compares the residuals of the
   measurements from a stated pre-horizon prediction with a null distribution
   simulated from that prediction on the same grid with the same errors.

Rejections of physical predictions come from the named-prediction test, which
returns one of four verdicts: compatible over the measured range; localized
incompatibility, with an interval for the boundary; whole-range
incompatibility; or abstention. The screens flag relations for testing; where
a fitted regime is sampled, the extrapolation screen also gives a descriptive
boundary estimate.

## Principal results

Original controls are the nine pre-1900 control groups as originally
generated; regenerated controls are the same groups regenerated with the
sampling and noise process of the later families; the same-generator set is a
set of later formulas generated by the same process as the pre-1900 corpus; the
untrained network is the same network with its initial random weights and no
training steps; metrological groups are calibration artefacts or instrument
transfer curves. Each of the 38 and 55 later families carries a primary
historical citation.
A synthetic departure family is a set of synthetic curves built by imposing one kind of
departure, such as saturation, a step or a kink, on a classical form; such
curves are used only for evaluation.

- The large encoder, a 20,908,547-parameter encoder trained alongside a
  training-only formula network, was trained on 880,896 point clouds from
  3,441 dimensionless reductions of pre-1900 laws (each formula rewritten in
  dimensionless one-dimensional form). Its query-time point student in
  `models/` has 9,868,672 parameters.
- Large encoder, original controls, 38 families from 1901–1950 vs nine
  pre-1900 groups: AUROC 0.921 [0.833, 0.994]; untrained network 0.589.
- Large encoder, original controls, 55 families from 1901–1996 vs nine
  pre-1900 groups: AUROC 0.925 [0.851, 0.982]; untrained network 0.649.
- Large encoder, regenerated controls (added after the primary results were
  known), 55 later vs nine pre-1900 groups: AUROC 0.857 [0.741, 0.945];
  untrained network 0.545.
- Large encoder, same-generator set of 1901–1950 formulas vs nine pre-1900
  groups: AUROC 0.536 [0.424, 0.643]; untrained network 0.594
  [0.436, 0.756].
- Extrapolation screen, 55 later vs 11 pre-1900 families:
  AUROC 0.888 [0.800, 0.959] with no learned representation; in the same test
  the compact encoder (a width-128 set transformer trained on 205 pre-1900
  point clouds from 40 families) gives 0.669 [0.534, 0.798].
- Compact encoder trained on pre-1900 laws vs the generic-function control
  encoder, 12 synthetic departure and 12 classical families: family AUROC 0.903
  [0.729, 1.000] vs 0.375 [0.167, 0.618].
- Extrapolation screen, 200 pre-1900 formula datasets and 30 synthetic
  departures: AUROC 0.88.
- Extrapolation screen at the calibrated threshold, 12 synthetic departure and 12
  classical families: true-positive rate 0.271 [0.073, 0.500]; 0/96 false
  positives at the 5%-targeted threshold.
- Comparison with successor predictions on identical data grids, that is
  rescoring the same measurements after replacing the pre-1900 prediction by
  the prediction of the theory that superseded it: all five pre-1900 predictions are rejected,
  and 4/5 successor predictions are not rejected on the same grid and error
  model; 5/5 wrong-shape and 5/5
  wrong-mechanism predictions remain rejected.
- Sixteen unmodified NIST measured series, 5 post-1900 vs 9 classical or
  metrological groups: learned (compact encoder) AUROC 0.62 [0.29, 0.89];
  extrapolation AUROC 0.78 [0.38, 1.00]; both intervals include 0.5. All 128
  scores from resampled copies are released without a threshold decision. The two highest learned
  scores belong to a standard-resistor record and a polymer-impedance curve.
- Boundary localization on 900 held-out simulations, with intervals
  calibrated separately at each sample size: 90% interval coverage 91.3%
  [89.3%, 93.0%].
- Simulated instrument effects at maximum severity: false-positive rate 0.40
  for saturation, 0.63 for censoring and at most 0.11 for all other tested
  effects.

Results are reduced at the law-family or mechanism-group level. The
machine-readable outputs, protocol files and hashes behind the reported
numbers are included under `results/` and `learned_v3/`; `results/evidence_manifest.json`
is the evidence ledger linking each reported number to its script and output.

## Repository layout

```text
learned_v3/      v3 architecture, training, evaluation and portable inference
models/          query-time point student of the large encoder (1899 horizon)
                 and its inference memory
engine/          statistical calibration, temporal audits, real-measurement
                 case construction and recomputation checks
casestudies/     historical and robustness experiments
data/            public inputs, generated evaluation point clouds and the
                 44-file NIST real-measurement inventory with hashes
results/         numerical outputs and evidence manifests
figures/         publication figures and the plot_*.py scripts that draw them
figure_scripts/  matplotlib generators for the remaining manuscript figures
protocols/       confirmatory protocol template and the historical-case test
                 objects, with their JSON schema
paper/           LaTeX sources, bibliography and compiled PDFs of the
                 manuscript and Supplementary Information
```

Files retained from the original 2026-08 release support the earlier detector.
The learned results reported in the paper come from the `epoch_1899_*` assets
and the `learned_v3/` pipeline. The earlier-detector scripts that rebuild its
corpus need two inputs that are not distributed here, the formula-graph node
file and the formula-to-point-cloud generator; pass them with `--master` and
`--gen-dataside` or the environment variables listed in
`engine/legacy_paths.py`.

## Portable learned inference

Install the runtime dependencies and score the included benchmark of 55
post-1900 law families:

```bash
python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
python learned_v3/inference.py \
  --input data/post1900_formula_pointclouds.npz \
  --output output/portable_post1900_scores.json
```

For a single dataset, pass a two-column CSV or whitespace-delimited text file.
The script applies the fixed per-cloud normalization and deterministic
64-point sampling, then returns the four calibrated distances (each a rank on
its pre-1900 calibration distribution) and their unweighted mean,
`one_class_ensemble`, which is the learned score.

## Real-measurement series

The sixteen NIST series are built from the downloaded files in
`data/real_measurement_benchmark/raw/` and scored with the compact encoder,
calibration and thresholds of the leave-family-out evaluation, without
retraining:

```bash
python engine/build_real_measurement_cases.py      # -> data/real_measurement_case_*.{npz,json}
python engine/evaluate_real_measurement_cases.py   # -> results/real_measurement_case_evaluation.json
python figures/plot_real_measurement_cases.py      # -> figures/sfigure_measured.*
```

Column semantics, the 1899 pre-horizon prediction and the mechanism group of
every series are declared in `engine/build_real_measurement_cases.py`.
`results/real_measurement_case_view_scores.csv` holds the raw learned and
extrapolation scores of every resampled or rescaled copy of a series.

## Model and data records

The query-time point student and its pre-1900 inference memory are stored in
`models/`, together with SHA-256 sidecars. The full training checkpoint and
large point-cloud/AST archives are distributed through the versioned archive:

- GitHub: https://github.com/ChenxiHeCam/Epistemic-probe-of-Classical-Horizons
- Zenodo, concept DOI (latest version): https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22138620

The archive for this release will be added to the Zenodo record on publication.

### Pre-1900 records of the compact encoder's corpus

In `data/pre1900_audited_records.jsonl`, the law family, date and citation of
each record come from the registry of dated pre-1900 sources
(`data/pre1900_registry.json`); the formula itself comes from a larger
machine-assembled formula graph, parts of which were generated with a language
model or constructed combinatorially, and was not individually checked against
the original source. The graph-edge fields (`edges_in`, `edges_out`) are
inherited from that graph and are not used by any script. The large encoder's training formulas and the
same-generator set come from the same graph.
